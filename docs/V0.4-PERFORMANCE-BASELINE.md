# v0.3.0 performance baseline and adaptive scheduler qualification

This baseline is captured from the exact `v0.3.0` tag, commit
`b4580d61cddadeeca3b99420a1aae2857b9de62e`. It is a diagnostic reference for
new performance work, not a release gate or a cross-machine guarantee.

Raw CSV, metadata, and complete benchmark summaries are retained in the
[research companion at pinned commit `af8bae2d0c32725c7d39a8e0bec6723a4be08b9c`](https://github.com/alex-1974/concurrency-d-research/tree/af8bae2d0c32725c7d39a8e0bec6723a4be08b9c/research/v0_3_release_baseline).
Both hosts ran the same release source, 4-worker/eight-wave end-to-end suite,
LDC release builds, 1,024 base iterations, four independent sessions, 20
measured pairs and five warmups per setting. Each arm validates an identical
checksum. Ratios below are paired elapsed-time ratios for the reusable
`parallelForAdaptive` arm divided by the matching reused Phobos arm. The
reported grain is selected by pooled p50 for each workload; it is not a
portable tuning recommendation.

## What the original baseline supports

The adaptive range path is effectively at Phobos median parity on the XPS for
these selected settings. Its pooled fine-work p95 is elevated, but the four
session p95 ratios at minimum grain 1 are 1.055, 1.227, 1.129 and 1.043. The
tail is therefore sensitive to session-level variation. On ARM64, the fine
workload is consistently about 3% slower than Phobos at its selected minimum
grain; the other workloads are at parity. The best fine-work minimum grain
differs sharply between the two hosts.

This baseline did not identify the cause or justify a scheduler redesign by
itself. It also does not support a universal target such as “beat Phobos.”
Treat the XPS p95 as a profiling clue, not an established cross-platform
regression.

## Split-on-steal candidate

PR #56 changes only fine `parallelForAdaptive(..., minGrainSize: 1, ...)`
scheduling. On the XPS 15 with LDC 1.42, 40 paired public-API samples per
workload and worker count at 1,024 items showed 36–39% lower p50 at eight
participants. At four participants, p50 gains ranged from 15% to 39%; the
four-worker heavy-prefix p95 was 7% higher. At 16,384 items, four-worker
heavy-suffix p50 and p95 were each about 6% higher, while other tested profiles
were near parity or faster.

Native ARM64/LDC 1.41 captures compared the same harness against v0.3.0 with
four participants. The pull-request run used 48 measured samples per workload
and arm. An extended run used eight sessions and 240 measured samples per
workload and arm. In the extended run, adaptive p50 ratios (candidate / v0.3.0)
ranged from 0.818 to 0.973 at 1,024 items and from 0.790 to 0.997 at 16,384
items. P95 ratios ranged from 0.763 to 0.957 and from 0.789 to 1.074,
respectively. The smaller run's heavy-prefix 16k p95 outlier did not persist in
the extended run. No tested ARM64 workload exceeded the 10% p95 review screen.
The [extended raw capture](https://github.com/alex-1974/concurrency-d/actions/runs/40561700792),
[PR #56](https://github.com/alex-1974/concurrency-d/pull/56), and
[implementation](https://github.com/alex-1974/concurrency-d/tree/863c0f19c3ee1f4b71ff0bd5afb81a590875cc57)
retain the reproducible harness and validation.

These measurements support integrating the candidate into `develop`, not a
universal speedup claim or an automatic v0.4.0 release. They cover one XPS and
one hosted ARM64 machine, four participant settings in the ARM64 comparison,
and a focused fine-adaptive matrix. Keep [ADR-0003](adr/0003-coalesce-idle-publish-notifications.md)
and [ADR-0005](adr/0005-keep-gc-owned-task-records.md) unchanged unless separate
measurements identify parking or task records as material bottlenecks.

## Tuned direct comparison with Phobos

PR #57 adds matched sweeps for `parallelFor` grain, `parallelForAdaptive`
minimum grain and Phobos `TaskPool.parallel` batch size at 1, 4, 16, 64 and 256.
All arms use the same checksummed callbacks, four total participants (including
the caller), eight reused waves and rotating arm order. The XPS capture has 80
measured samples per workload and arm; the native ARM64/LDC 1.41 capture has
240. For each workload, the table reports the concurrency arm and Phobos batch
with the lowest pooled p50, plus the p50 and p95 ratios for that selected
concurrency arm. Selection and evaluation use the same samples, so the winning
ratios are exploratory and should guide profiling rather than serve as an
unbiased product guarantee.

| Host | Item count | Best concurrency / Phobos settings | Candidate / Phobos p50 ratio | p95 ratio |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|
| XPS 15, LDC 1.42 | 1,024 | workload-specific | 1.06–1.52 | 1.00–1.78 |
| XPS 15, LDC 1.42 | 16,384 | workload-specific | 1.06–1.18 | 1.00–1.43 |
| ARM64 Neoverse-N2, LDC 1.41 | 1,024 | workload-specific | 1.29–2.12 | 1.18–2.06 |
| ARM64 Neoverse-N2, LDC 1.41 | 16,384 | workload-specific | 1.04–1.40 | 1.04–1.37 |

These results show two distinct regimes. Larger chunks bring the 16,384-item
profiles close to Phobos on both hosts, while the 1,024-item and especially
short-uniform profiles still pay substantially more scheduler overhead. The
best grain also changes by host and work distribution, so no single default or
automatic workload classifier is justified yet. Against the v0.3.0 adaptive
arm at grain 1, the new candidate's best p50 ratios range from 0.73 to 1.04 on
the XPS and 0.79 to 1.00 on ARM64.

The next optimization should profile the short-task path to separate executor
startup/scope costs, range-claim synchronization and callback time, then change
one measured bottleneck at a time. Keep these benchmark settings and the raw
captures with the [qualification run](https://github.com/alex-1974/concurrency-d/actions/runs/40566914191),
[PR #57](https://github.com/alex-1974/concurrency-d/pull/57), and the
[maintained benchmark harness](https://github.com/alex-1974/concurrency-d/tree/b5cea1aef503c16c6101f1aab68da51ca36fc6ea/benchmarks/split-on-steal).
